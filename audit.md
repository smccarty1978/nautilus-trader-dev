## Critical findings

### [C1/State] strategy.py vs compression_vwap_study.py — Research-to-Production Signal Mismatch (Raw vs. Confirmed Flips)

There is a major disconnect in entry signal logic and timing between the live backtest strategy (`strategy.py`) and the offline study (`compression_vwap_study.py`).
1. **Confirmation Check Missing:** The production strategy checks only the raw regime flip:
   ```python
   # Gate 2: must be a long flip
   if not tf.regime_flipped or tf.regime != 1:
       return
   ```
   It enters immediately at the close of the flip bar. However, the offline study operates on data from `v2_feature_snapshots` (generated by `collector.py`), which filters for events that have been *confirmed* on the subsequent bar (`bar1` makes a new HH/LL and closes in the trade direction).
2. **Timing Shift:** Because the study relies on `signal_time` from the collector, which is defined as the close of the confirmation bar `bar1` (T = flip_close + 60s), the study's "A flip-bar entry" actually enters at the close of `bar1` (1 minute after the raw flip). The strategy, however, enters at the close of the raw flip bar itself (T = flip_close).
   
As a result, the backtest runner is trading unconfirmed raw flips at T, whereas the study optimized confirmed flips entering at T + 60s.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Modify `strategy.py` to defer entry until confirmation is established: hold the pending flip state for one bar, verify that the subsequent bar makes a new HH (for longs) or LL (for shorts) relative to the flip bar, and close in the trade direction. Submit the entry order at the close of this confirmation bar.

### [C1/State] compression_vwap_study.py:176-184 — Shifted Confirmation Logic and Late Entry in Offline Study

The offline study script `compression_vwap_study.py` contains index calculation bugs that shift the confirmation check and entry timing 1 minute late:
```python
# bar1 confirm: flip bar opens at T-60s, bar1 opens at T
fb = midx.get(T - 60 * NS, -1)
b1 = midx.get(T, -1)
if fb >= 0 and b1 >= 0:
    if d == 1:
        confirm[k] = mh[b1] > mh[fb] and mc[b1] > mo[b1]
```
Since `T = signal_time` is the close of the confirmation bar `bar1` (e.g., `10:01:00`), `T - 60s = 10:00:00` represents the open of `bar1` (not the flip bar, which opens at `09:59:00`). Thus:
- `fb` retrieves the index of `bar1` (confirmation bar).
- `b1` retrieves the index of `bar2` (the bar *after* confirmation).
- `confirm[k]` checks whether `bar2` confirms `bar1` (`high[bar2] > high[bar1]`) rather than checking if `bar1` confirms `flip_bar`.
- The B-entry anchor `T_b = T + 60 * NS` evaluates the entry at the close of `bar2` (`10:02:00`) instead of `bar1` (`10:01:00`).

This shifts the confirmation validation and the B-entry execution 1 minute late relative to the intended logic.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Change lines 177-178 in `studies/v_a_excursion_regime/compression_vwap_study.py` to:
```python
fb = midx.get(T - 120 * NS, -1)  # flip bar open timestamp
b1 = midx.get(T - 60 * NS, -1)   # bar1 open timestamp
```
And adjust the B-entry anchor `T_b` to equal `T` (which is the close of `bar1`).

### [F1] strategy.py:226-227, 243 — RTH Boundary Pre-market Leakage & Last-Bar Rejection

The RTH filter in `strategy.py` uses the contemporaneous 1s bar close timestamp `ts` (the bar that triggers the 1m aggregation close) to evaluate session boundaries, leading to two bugs:
1. **Pre-market Leakage:** The first bar of RTH (e.g., `09:30:00` to `09:31:00`) is completed when the 1s bar at `09:30:01` arrives. However, the pre-market bar covering `09:29:00` to `09:30:00` also completes when the `09:30:01` 1s bar arrives. Since `ts = 09:30:01 ET` is inside the RTH window (`_RTH_START <= et.time() < _RTH_END`), `is_rth` is evaluated as `True` for this pre-market bar. This leaks pre-market signals into the strategy.
2. **Last-Bar Rejection:** The final bar of RTH covers `15:59:00` to `16:00:00`. It is completed when the 1s bar at `16:00:01` ET arrives. At `ts = 16:00:01`, `et.time()` is greater than `_RTH_END` (16:00:00), so `is_rth` evaluates to `False`. Thus, the strategy rejects the last RTH bar.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Evaluate RTH status based on the closed 1m bar's timestamp (`closed_1m["ts_event"]` representing the open time of the bar) rather than the triggering 1s bar close time `ts`. E.g., `is_rth = _RTH_START <= _to_et(closed_1m["ts_event"]).time() < _RTH_END`.

### [E3/State] strategy.py:467-486 — State and Order Desynchronization on Hard Exit

In `_exit_all_market` (triggered by the 2-hour hard position guard), the strategy submits a `Market` order with `TimeInForce.FOK` and immediately calls `self._record_trade(reason)`. This resets all active trade state variables (`_entry_px`, `_entry_atr`, `_entry_ts`, `_t1_filled`) and clears the resting stop/limit order IDs, assuming the trade is successfully closed.

However, since the order is submitted as a `FOK` (Fill-or-Kill) order, if it is rejected or fails to fill immediately, the portfolio position will remain open. Because all tracking variables have been reset and resting orders cancelled, the strategy will:
1. Remain in a non-flat portfolio state indefinitely (preventing any future entries since `self.portfolio.is_flat` is checked).
2. Fail to monitor or exit the remaining open position (since target and stop orders were cancelled and the active state was cleared).

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Track the exit order ID (e.g., `self._pending_exit`) instead of calling `_record_trade` and resetting state immediately. Move `_record_trade` and the state resets to `on_order_filled` after verifying that the exit order ID has been filled.

## Warnings

### [E3] run_backtest.py:88 — Optimistic Stop-Market Fills in Engine Configuration

The backtest engine is configured with `bar_execution=True` and `bar_adaptive_high_low_ordering=True`. While the adaptive ordering provides a more realistic intra-bar execution model, NautilusTrader's default execution engine fills stop-market orders at the exact trigger price. This results in overly optimistic backtest performance metrics (no slippage). 

The user attempts to correct this analytically in `run_sweep.py` by applying a 1-tick slippage to stop-loss fills, but the direct account reports generated by the engine (printed in `run_backtest.py`) remain optimistic.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Configure a custom fill model or execution model for the simulated venue in `run_backtest.py` to natively simulate slippage on stop orders, or document that the engine-generated report is untrusted for PnL.

### [E3] run_sweep.py:86 — Inaccurate Sweep PnL for Hard Exits

The sweep aggregation script calculates PnL using a simplified analytical formula based on the trade's `exit_reason`. For trades that exit via `max_hold`, it assigns a flat PnL of `-2 * COMM` (assuming zero price change). 

However, since `trades.parquet` does not record the actual exit price, the sweep script cannot calculate the true PnL of hard exits, which could be positive or negative depending on the market price at exit.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Modify `_record_trade` in `strategy.py` to record the actual `exit_px` (retrieved from the fill event of the exit order). Then update `run_sweep.py` to compute the PnL of `max_hold` exits using the recorded `exit_px` and `entry_px`.

### [E5] run_backtest.py:58-59 — Strategy is Active and Trades During Lead-In Warmup

The backtest runner specifies a lead-in period of 5 days (`load_start` is 5 days prior to the start of the year). During this lead-in period, the strategy is fully active and will receive bars, compute indicators, and submit orders. While the runner filters out trade records outside the target year for post-run analysis, lead-in trades can modify the account balance and margin state prior to the formal start of the backtest year.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
Add a timestamp check in `_check_entry` to prevent the strategy from submitting new entry orders before the actual backtest year start (e.g., `if ts < yr_start_ns: return`).

## Notes

### [A1] strategy.py:236-243 — One-Second Lag in 1m Bar Closing

In `TF1m.update`, the 1-minute bin close condition `b != self._bin` triggers when the first 1s bar of the *next* minute arrives. For example, the 1m bar covering `10:00:00` to `10:01:00` (which completes at `10:01:00`) is only returned as closed when the 1s bar ending at `10:01:01` is processed. 

This introduces a 1-second delay in signal evaluation (evaluating the `10:01:00` close at `10:01:01`). While this is safe and does not introduce look-ahead bias, it represents a minor delay/lag in trade execution.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
No action required as it is conservative, but be aware that the strategy trades 1 second late relative to a standard bar close.

### [G2] strategy.py:98-123 — Discontinuous Timeframe Aggregation on Gaps

The `TF1m.update` method does not generate empty 1m bars if there are gaps in the incoming 1s bar feed (e.g., during low-liquidity overnight periods). Instead, it silently skips empty minutes. 

This causes the rolling 30-bar window (`self._window_30m`) and recursive indicators (ATR/EMAs) to use the last 30 *active* bars rather than 30 *wall-clock* minutes. During RTH, NQ liquidity makes this negligible, but it can affect overnight indicator stabilization.

**Recommended fix (do not apply):**
If strict temporal alignment is desired, check for gaps between the current bin `b` and `self._bin` in `update`, and insert empty/flat 1m bars to fill the gap.
