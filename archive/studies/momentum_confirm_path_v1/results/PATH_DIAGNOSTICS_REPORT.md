## Final Recommendations

### 1. Is there a catastrophic SL level that improves the strategy?

**No.** Best is SL = 2.0 ATR with avg Δ -$1.33/trade across 6 cells, only 2/6 positive. Tighter SLs are worse because winner-tail MAE distribution is wide:
- Median winner MAE: 0.50 ATR
- 90th percentile winner MAE: 1.36 ATR
- ~22% of winners hit MAE >= 1.0 ATR before turning positive
- ~7% of winners hit MAE >= 1.5 ATR

Even SL = 2.0 stops 16% of trades and ~2.5% of original winners. The cost of cutting those winners outweighs the savings from cutting losers.

A SL = 2.0 ATR could be defended as a **safety/risk-control overlay** (not as an edge improvement) — it caps single-trade loss without materially changing economics.

### 2. Can losers be cut earlier without killing winners?

**No, not robustly.** The closest to a positive aggregate is "exit if PnL < -0.50 ATR at 180s" (+$2.65 avg, 2/6 positive). But it only saves 20-26% of losers and sacrifices 9-15% of eventual winners. The winner-vs-loser path overlap at 60-180s is too large:
- At 60s, 42% of eventual losers are still currently positive
- At 120s, 41% of eventual losers are still currently positive
- At 180s, 43% of eventual losers are still currently positive

You can't reliably distinguish them at short horizons. Real separation only happens at 600s+ (93% of winners positive vs 55% of losers), but by then the regime exit is approaching anyway.

### 3. Is regime-exit giving back too much MFE?

**Yes — but capturing it costs too much winner-tail.** Median MFE capture ratio is -0.46 (median trade gives back ALL its peak MFE plus some). Mean is -3.06 (huge outliers).

But the MFE distribution is heavy-tailed. Mean MFE 2.3 ATR, but Q90 winners reach 3.0+ ATR. The trades giving back ALL their MFE are the losers (which had transient MFE that didn't materialize). The winners DO capture significant MFE — winners' median MAE stays at 0.5 ATR even at 1200s while their MFE climbs to 3.8 ATR.

### 4. Do trailing exits improve MFE capture?

**No.** Every trailing rule shows negative Δ on aggregate (-$2 to -$10/trade). Even the best (1.5 act / 0.75 give) gives -$2.06 avg, 2/6 positive. Trailing fires too often (47-49% of trades) and exits at giveback that turns out to be a temporary pullback.

The "winner running" pattern of this strategy depends on holding through retracements that look like reversals at the time. Trailing breaks that.

### 5. Are partials worth testing in NT?

**No.** All three partial models show negative Δ aggregate (-$4 to -$6). Even the best (Model C, 1/2 @ +0.75) is -$4.15 avg, 2/6 positive. The trade fee on the partial half is significant relative to the small partial profit, and the remainder loses the full-position edge in winners.

### 6. Which 1-2 rules deserve NT runtime validation?

**None as a clear edge improvement.** Every overlay tested fails the success criteria (helps in 2+ years, PF > 1.10).

**ONE candidate as a safety overlay**: **Catastrophic SL = 2.0 ATR**, valued for risk control rather than edge improvement. It:
- Stops only 14-17% of trades
- Stops only ~2.5% of eventual winners
- Reduces 2026 max DD significantly
- Costs ~$1.33/trade on average
- Could be NT-validated as a "safety net" if you want max-trade-loss bounded

**Conclusion**: This strategy's edge IS the regime-exit hold profile. Adding stops, time exits, trailing, or partials all damage it. The path diagnostics confirm the strategy is structurally a "many small losses, occasional very large winners with patient holding" profile. Any attempt to "improve" via shortened holds or tighter stops cuts into the winner tail more than it saves on losers.

The cross-year inversion in EVERY tested rule family (helps 2026, hurts 2024-2025) is the strongest evidence: there's no rule that's robustly better across regimes. The strategy as-is is what it is.

**If you want to deploy this, deploy it without any overlay.** The 3-yr aggregate (PF 1.03 V_A, 1.02 V_B) is what you get. 2024-2025 are profitable, 2026 is loss-making — that's the volatility signature, not something fixable with exit-rule tuning.
